# NowTrendIn — Copyright & Data-Use Position (headlines, facts, derived scores)
**Version 1.0 · 2026-08-10 · Adopted by Chairman ruling. Supporting authority
assembled at the Chairman's direction; this is a POSITION STATEMENT with cited
doctrine, not a legal opinion — the standing recommendation for an outside-counsel
memo (rights register §H.3) remains open and this document is its foundation.**

## 1. The position (Chairman's ruling, 2026-08-10, recorded verbatim in substance)

What NowTrendIn ingests from publisher feeds is **headlines, public names, and
common words — facts and identifiers, not expressive text**. We do not take
articles' text; we do not republish content; we do not claim any publisher's work
as our own. What we SELL is our own product: scores and measurements generated by
our own models from aggregate observation. Additionally, as ruled: **public RSS
headlines are a different thing from API access** — a publisher's open syndication
feed, offered to the public for consumption, is not the same access class as its
licensed API product, and our register documents the two separately.

## 2. Supporting doctrine (per the reviewed authority)

Source reviewed and incorporated at the Chairman's direction: Oberheiden P.C.
(federal-lawyer.com), "Is Data Scraping for AI Considered 'Fair Use'?" — an
AI-litigation practice explainer of the U.S. framework. Key doctrine it states:

- **Copyright protects "original works of authorship fixed in a tangible medium of
  expression" — it "DOES NOT protect facts, data, or ideas"** (quoting Princeton's
  formulation of the U.S. Copyright Act's scope). Only the creative EXPRESSION of
  ideas is protected.
- **Fair use** is a separate, additional doctrine excusing certain uses of even
  protected expression — weighing purpose/character (transformativeness),
  nature, amount used, and market effect.

## 3. Application to NowTrendIn's pipeline (the mapped facts)

| Our practice | Doctrine it sits under |
|---|---|
| Titles/headline metadata only; never article body text | Headlines function as factual identifiers of events; short phrases carry thin-to-no copyright; the FACTS a headline reports are categorically unprotected |
| Topic names, tickers, public names, counts | Facts and data — outside the Copyright Act's scope |
| Served product = our own scores/components from our own models | Original work of OUR authorship; even where any input were protected expression, the use is transformative measurement, uses minimal amounts, and substitutes for no publisher's market (the fair-use factors) |
| Direct official RSS, no paywall circumvention, no aggregator relabeling | Publisher-offered public distribution channel — distinct from licensed API access (Chairman's documented distinction) |
| §17 display rule: sources shown only where they contribute; attribution preserved | No passing-off; provenance disclosed |

## 4. What this position does NOT claim (kept honest, per the App Annie rule)

- It does not claim a court has blessed this exact pipeline; scraping/fair-use law
  is actively litigated, and the cited authority is a law firm's explainer, not our
  counsel's opinion on our facts.
- It does not extend to article text, images, or content reproduction — which the
  pipeline does not do and must never do.
- It does not resolve CONTRACT questions (a site's terms of service are a separate
  body of law from copyright) — that is precisely what the counsel memo should
  cover, using this document as its brief.
- Sources remain governed by §16 gates and the rights register regardless: a
  documented right stays stronger than a doctrinal position wherever obtainable.
