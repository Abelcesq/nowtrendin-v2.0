Part 2 — What buyers actually check, in order
This is the real answer to &quot;what do they seek.&quot; It is largely specified for free by the FISD Alternative Data Council (a buy-side-led body inside SIIA, active and publishing; its DDQ, tear sheets and five Learning Points are public, some on GitHub).
⚠️ Do not cite IDSO. It was a real standards body in 2018–19 but appears dormant — idso.org now redirects to an unrelated organisation. Cite FISD.
1 · Point-in-time integrity. FISD's definition: data must &quot;reflect, at each Point-In-Time, what was known and when it was known.&quot; Look-ahead bias is &quot;using data that was not yet known on the date being analyzed.&quot; Survivorship bias is a universe that &quot;only represents the present.&quot; There is no published minimum number of years. The closest thing to a threshold is J.P. Morgan's guidance that under ~50 observations is &quot;typically less useful,&quot; and the contractual default in the SBAI Standardised Trial Data License Agreement, which is &quot;full available history&quot; less a commercially sensitive window. A vendor drip-feeding a two-year sample is already off-standard.
2 · Data lineage. This is the hardest gate — 90% of FISD webinar attendees named it the most difficult requirement to satisfy. Vendors must document whether data &quot;was backfilled,&quot; whether &quot;missing values were interpolated,&quot; whether &quot;values were altered for schema alignment,&quot; whether &quot;outliers were removed,&quot; with audit trails logging when and by whom.
3 · Rights to redistribute. The FISD DDQ requires contracts evidencing the legal right to resell or license the data and any derived products. It probes web-scraping method, proxy use, IP anonymisation, and compliance with robots.txt and terms of service specifically. Its organising principle is excluding data from &quot;breach of fiduciary duty, breach of contract or deceptive behavior.&quot; &quot;We scraped it and nobody complained&quot; is not a right.
4 · PII — an instant kill, not a negotiation. Nasdaq's data team: &quot;We do not even want to see data that contains PII and nor do our clients. It is not worth the compliance risk.&quot;
5 · MNPI. Vendors must diligence origin to ensure no material non-public information.
6 · Identifier mapping, at ROW level. FISD's Identifiers Learning Point names the accepted set — ticker, ISIN, FIGI, SEDOL, CUSIP, PermID, RIC — and states the requirement plainly: &quot;a security identifier is essential to be included, for example, for each row of data provided.&quot; Unmapped data isn't rejected on principle; the buyer prices in the mapping labour and the dataset stops clearing its hurdle. Nasdaq rejected a geolocation dataset on exactly that basis.
7 · Coverage breadth and stability. Eagle Alpha's stated quant threshold is a universe of 75+ tickers. Coverage that drifts over the history invalidates the backtest even when the signal is real.
8 · Economics, which is a separate gate from statistics. One manager told AIMA it requires alpha net of acquisition, cleaning, structuring and analysis costs. 1798 Alternatives targets at least 10x return relative to all-in cost. A real signal that doesn't clear the economics is rejected while being perfectly valid.
9 · Vendor permanency. Neudata's process explicitly includes &quot;company background and permanency risk checks.&quot; A one-person vendor is a documented risk item, and buyers hedge it by spreading payments across the term rather than paying upfront.
The App Annie rule — the most important compliance fact for us
SEC v. App Annie (Sept 2021) was the first SEC enforcement action against an alternative data provider: $10M from the company, $300,000 plus a three-year officer-and-director bar for the co-founder. The violation was not collecting bad data. It was misdescribing its own methodology and controls to buyers. Every answer we put in a DDQ becomes a securities-law exposure. This is the strongest possible argument for the honest-absence discipline we already run — and against ever publishing an accuracy number we cannot reproduce.
Part 3 — The hard truth about selling a score
The weight of evidence says sophisticated quant funds do not buy opaque scores as a primary input. Deloitte: leading firms favour &quot;raw, minimally-processed data, allowing internal transformation for specific economic problems rather than vendor-curated signals.&quot; Jane Street staffs feature engineering in-house and tests whether a dataset is &quot;additive to our modeling.&quot;
Four mechanical reasons, all downstream of Part 2 rather than snobbery:
A score cannot be orthogonalised against the buyer's existing factor book unless they can decompose it. &quot;Additive to our modeling&quot; is the test, and a black box can't be tested.
A score cannot be reconstructed point-in-time if the model was retrained — the historical score series then isn't what would have been observable. That is look-ahead bias smuggled in through the model.
A score's revisions are model revisions, colliding head-on with the lineage requirement to document whether &quot;values were altered.&quot;
A score gives the buyer no way to answer &quot;why is it predictive,&quot; which is a first-order test.
But this is not a dead end, and the nuance is where our opportunity lives. Only about 38% of buy-side firms want truly raw data; the majority want cleaned, normalised data with embedded tags to securities, industries and timestamps, and only ~17% need no supporting services. So the commercially survivable structure is clear:
Ship the components AND the score, both with full point-in-time vintages, publish the transformation logic to FISD lineage standard, and let the buyer reproduce the score.
And the honest strategic reframe: the Rich List firms buy raw data because they employ 750 engineers. Our score is most valuable precisely to buyers who don't — smaller funds, family offices, RIAs, corporate strategy and IR teams, and sell-side research. Positioning against Citadel's Data Strategies Group is positioning against our own strength.
One more finding we should not flinch from
Industry data indicates card-transaction and web-scraped data are the most used and most highly rated, while social/sentiment data has the most providers and ranks lower for analytical value. That is our category. It means the differentiation cannot be &quot;we have sentiment data.&quot; It has to be the point-in-time rigour and the provenance — which is, conveniently, exactly what we have been building.

Part 5 — What would fail a DDQ today
Blunt, because the App Annie rule means overstating readiness is worse than admitting gaps.
The accuracy numbers. Seven published figures spanning 0%–54.5% (audit finding A4). A buyer asking &quot;what is your hit rate&quot; gets seven answers. This must be resolved into scoped, reproducible, per-ledger figures before any external conversation.
Crypto grades a signal with zero money input. crypto_accuracy_ledger has been scoring against a money leg that does not exist. Fix or withdraw the ledger.
A live credential sits in plaintext in engine CLAUDE.md line 100. Any DDQ security question fails on this. Rotate it — this is a today item, independent of any sales plan.
No PII or MNPI policy document exists. Both are required DDQ answers.
No row-level security identifier.
Coverage count is unknown against the 75-ticker quant threshold. We have never counted.
No null model for the 365-day lead window (audit finding A5) — a buyer will ask what the random-chance baseline is, and &quot;we haven't computed it&quot; is a stopping answer.
Single-founder permanency risk is a literal Neudata check item.
AGENT_CHARTER.md and DATA_BUILDING_BLOCKS.md have never been uploaded, so we cannot yet describe our own agent inventory completely.

Phase 3 — Sell to the right tier first
Do not open with Citadel, Millennium, D.E. Shaw or Point72. They are addressable, but they buy raw data, run 700-dataset funnels, and will find every Phase 0 gap. Approach them after Phases 0–2, and even then expect a long cycle — Deloitte puts discovery-to-institutionalisation at two to three years.
Open instead with the segment that buys processed signals because it lacks in-house quant capacity: smaller funds, family offices, RIAs, corporate strategy and IR teams, and sell-side research. That is where a score is the product rather than a liability.
When we do approach the large funds, the sales motion is three-sided: central data sourcing = the contracting counterparty and negotiator (Point72's own posting: &quot;negotiating terms, and securing approvals from investment teams&quot;); quant research or the sector data-science group = the technical approver; a named PM or pod = the economic sponsor. At a pod shop you need all three, and the sponsor is what makes it happen.
One asymmetry to exploit: a large quant manager told AIMA that ~80% of new dataset discoveries came from investment research testing a specific thesis, and only 20% from prospecting. We are far more likely to be found because a fund is already asking our question than because they are browsing. So publish the methodology and the question we answer, publicly and precisely.
Phase 4 — Pricing, when we get there
Published bands: overall under $25k to $500k+/year, with most datasets in the $25k–$150k range; sentiment specifically skews low (~30% under $25k). Average dataset revenue is ~$1.1M/year; elite ones exceed $20M. Buyers average ~$1.6M annual spend across ~20 subscriptions.
Price against the 10x all-in-cost hurdle, not against our own costs. And note the trap: 62% of buyers cite high prices as the primary adoption obstacle, while the retention above 90% means the whole commercial problem is winning the first evaluation, not holding the account.
Part 7 — The one-line strategy
We are not competing on having sentiment data — that category is crowded and rated low. We are competing on being the sentiment dataset that a compliance team can actually approve and a quant can actually reproduce. Point-in-time from the start, lineage documented, rights written down, identifiers on every row, methodology published, absences shown honestly.
Everything in Phase 0 and Phase 1 serves that sentence, and none of it requires a customer, a fundraise, or a single new data source.
